A little about how I work

Every problem - and every person - deserves a closer look.

I've always believed there can be something valuable beneath the surface. I like to listen, understand what is really needed, and invest the time to help bring it into focus.

Follow the story ↓

01 - Listen first

People are more than the moment they're having.

At EarthLink and Verizon Wireless, I began in technical support and was promoted to the retention queue because I could de-escalate calls and help customers who were ready to cancel. Most days brought a steady stream of angry or frustrated people, so I learned to stay calm, listen carefully, and focus on what might make a real difference to each customer. I also helped train teammates.

Those jobs were also where I built much of my technical foundation: at EarthLink, a major internet service provider, I supported customers as DSL was emerging; at Verizon Wireless, I worked through the early growth of iPhones and smartphones. I was regularly assigned to new-acquisition groups when Verizon acquired smaller companies, helping their customers understand the transition. Supporting people through new technology and change strengthened my troubleshooting skills and taught me to make unfamiliar things easier to navigate.

At the Public Health Institute, I contacted randomly selected California residents and invited them to participate in anonymous surveys about sensitive health topics. The conversations required patience, discretion, and respect. I was also effective using LanguageLine interpreters to conduct surveys with residents across language differences.

At Californians Against Waste, I called members to explain recycling legislation and encourage support through donations and communication with state legislators. I also helped edit and format grant requests and legislative bill drafts.

Different goals, same starting point: hear the person in front of you and make the next step clear.

02 - Build what's needed

Sometimes the work is still taking shape.

I joined GO-Biz when it was a new department as its first formal rank-and-file hire and one of only two administrative staff. As Executive Operations, Procurement & IT Support Coordinator, I supported five Deputy Directors - appointed by the Governor serving at the time - and their teams as the organization grew.

The work crossed many boundaries. I helped establish procurement procedures and purchasing practices, tracked bills and purchases, prepared executive and public-meeting materials, and kept website information and constituent-request logs current. I also served as a hands-on IT contact, preparing equipment and meeting technology, troubleshooting issues, and coordinating with the Governor's Office IT team.

When a business owner called looking for help, I worked to connect them with useful California resources. When the department needed something unfamiliar, I learned what was required and followed it through. That combination of practical problem-solving and service helped a growing office keep moving.

I created the complete California State Economic Survey and Incentive Comparison, published in August 2015, bringing together what I had learned at GO-Biz about California's economy, business resources, regional strengths, educational opportunities, and manufacturing incentives. I developed the research and wrote the content myself. Read the published research.

03 - Make the process clearer

A good solution can keep helping long after it's built.

At the California Infrastructure and Economic Development Bank, I was the sole administrative staff member supporting Deputy Directors, who were appointed by the Governor serving at the time, along with Loan Officers and SBFC leadership. IBank helps California communities and eligible organizations finance infrastructure and economic development. My work spanned procurement, contracts, executive travel, public meetings, and frontline IT support.

I saw that applicants needed forms they could access through the IBank website, but the materials were Word documents. I taught myself Adobe LiveCycle Designer and converted the forms into digital versions ready to be posted online. I also created the Non-General Fund Addendum, which IBank has added to since then. The forms helped make application materials easier for people to access.

Examples of the posted forms: General Fund Addendum (2015 version, PDF) · Non-General Fund Addendum (PDF) · ISRF financing pre-application (2025 version, PDF).

I'm drawn to solutions that make a complicated process easier for the next person.

04 - Find the cause

A lasting fix starts with understanding the real problem.

At Gainwell, my work grew from supporting Medi-Cal providers, to Tier 2 help desk support for CalWIN and later CalSAWS, and then to ServiceNow administration and development for CalSAWS. That path brought me from helping people use the systems to improving the systems themselves.

At Gainwell, I developed and implemented ServiceNow applications, modules, and automated workflows for the CalSAWS statewide eligibility system. I customized forms, fields, lists, views, and UI policies, configured tables and relationships, and tested solutions to catch defects before deployment.

I worked with stakeholders to understand requirements and improve the platform. For the CC Data Request process, I partnered with the staff submitting requests and the team handling them to address unfulfilled or late requests. I created training videos for new and existing processes, tailored to the county, consortium, or team using them.

As the primary trainer and technical subject-matter expert for the other ServiceNow developers, I created onboarding materials, reviewed workflow designs, and helped establish consistent development standards. I also worked one-on-one with managers and team leads to build dashboards and reports that gave them clearer operational visibility.

A solution is stronger when people can understand it, use it, and build on it.
